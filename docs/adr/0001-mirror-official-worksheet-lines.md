# ADR 0001 — Calculators mirror the official worksheet line by line

**Status:** Accepted — 2026-08-17

## Context

FairShare's CS-42 and CS-42-S calculators were written as compact arithmetic — an algebraic rearrangement of the worksheets that produced a payer and an amount. An audit against the AOC's own Excel workbooks (Form CS-42 Rev. 5/2022, Form CS-42-S Eff. 6/2023) found the schedule table identical but the arithmetic drifting from the state's sheet:

- CS-42 subtracted the $981 self-support reserve from *gross* income; the form subtracts it from *adjusted* income (line 2).
- CS-42 had no $50 minimum obligation on line 12 and did not model line 13's "no gross income → $0".
- Both forms used .NET's default banker's rounding; Excel's `ROUND` rounds halves away from zero. On CS-42-S about a quarter of random cases came out $1 off, a few up to $50 off (a percentage share sitting on a .xx5 boundary), and the repo's own regression test expected 662 where the workbook says 661.
- Percentage shares were rounded independently; the workbooks round one parent and give the other the remainder (CS-42 rounds the plaintiff, CS-42-S the defendant), and CS-42 gives the defendant the remainder of line 8 too.
- CS-42-S was computed as a difference of two net obligations through extra intermediate roundings rather than as lines 3 → 14.
- Income above the schedule surfaced as an "unexpected error"; only payer and amount were returned, so nothing could be checked against the paper form.

These are court forms. A user compares FairShare's answer with a printed worksheet or the clerk's spreadsheet; being a dollar off is a credibility problem, not a rounding footnote.

## Decision

1. **The official workbook is the reference implementation.** Each calculator walks its form top to bottom, computing every numbered line with the workbook's own formula, and returns all lines (`CalculationResult.Lines`) alongside payer and amount.
2. **Excel rounding semantics everywhere:** `MidpointRounding.AwayFromZero` for every `ROUND`; percentage shares as one rounded parent plus the remainder, in the workbook's direction for each form; CS-42 line 8 defendant = line 7 − plaintiff's line 8.
3. **Line semantics exactly as printed:** SSR on line 2 adjusted income; the $50 minimum; "no gross income → $0"; CS-42-S line 14 = the higher line-13 amount in that parent's column (ties to the defendant); a tie or non-positive order is reported as "no net transfer" (empty payer, $0) while the lines keep the raw workbook values.
4. **Schedule lookup as the workbook does it:** `MAX(250, ROUND(CAGI/50)*50)`; negative CAGI floors to the $250 row; a bracket above $30,000 is a validation error (`INCOME_ABOVE_SCHEDULE`) rather than an invented amount, because the guidelines leave that to the court and the workbook shows `#N/A`.
5. **Deliberate deviations from Excel, and only these:** when combined adjusted income is $0 the shares are 0 (the workbook shows `#DIV/0!`); the payer on CS-42 is chosen from the primary-custody flag (the workbook fills both columns and leaves the choice to the court).
6. **Golden cases are read from the workbook, not computed:** `src/FairShare.Tests/Domain/Golden/*.json` are generated by driving the official workbooks through Excel and reading each line back; the calculators are pinned to them.

## Consequences

- Some results move by a dollar (662 → 661 in the CS-42-S regression case); low-income CS-42 payers now owe the $50 minimum instead of $0; CS-42 payers with preexisting deductions get the correct SSR cap.
- The API response grows a `lines[]` array; the SPA can render the worksheet, and a workbook export can be verified cell by cell.
- The code is longer and reads like the form rather than like tidy algebra. That is the point: a reviewer can put the workbook next to the calculator and check each line.
- Any future form is expected to follow the same discipline: implement `BuildWorksheet`, add golden cases from that form's official workbook.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the algebraic form, fix rounding only.** Cheaper, but the intermediate roundings still differ from the sheet in some cases and no line values are available.
- **Clamp above-schedule incomes to the top bracket.** Produces a number the state's workbook would not, and hides a case that legally needs a judge.
- **Compute in floating point.** Rejected; whole-dollar decimal arithmetic with explicit rounding is what the workbook does.
